The Dissertation Title Page

Every element, in order, with the formatting rules that vary and the one that never does.

A dissertation title page carries the full title, your name or student number, the degree the work is submitted for, the institution and department, and the month and year of submission. It shows no page number, though it is counted silently as page i, and it carries no decoration unless your handbook permits it.

The one rule that never varies is that your handbook wins. Title pages are the most locally specified part of a dissertation, because they identify the submission administratively. Where this guide and your handbook disagree, follow the handbook.

A dissertation title page example is useful precisely because the page is formulaic. There is nothing to invent and everything to get exactly right, which makes it an easy mark to keep and an easy one to drop. This guide sets out the elements, the conventions, and the errors we correct most often on the work we review.

The elements, in order

Two of those need comment. The degree line should give the exact programme title as it appears in your handbook, so "submitted in partial fulfilment of the requirements for the degree of MSc International Business" rather than "for my masters". And the date is the month and year of submission, not of starting, and not the academic year.

REMOTE WORKING POLICY AND VOLUNTARY TURNOVER
IN UK PROFESSIONAL SERVICES FIRMS

Student Number: 21045872

Submitted in partial fulfilment of the requirements
for the degree of MSc Human Resource Management

Department of Management
University of Manchester

September 2026

Word count: 14,892

That layout is centred, uses one font throughout and separates the blocks with white space rather than with rules or boxes. It carries no logo, because most institutions reserve their crest for official documents and a number explicitly prohibit its use on student work. Check before adding one.

The formatting rules that catch people out

The page numbering convention is the one that produces the most confusion. The title page is counted in the numbering sequence but does not display a number. In practice that means your front matter runs i, ii, iii with the title page silently occupying i, so your abstract usually appears as ii or iii. Achieving that in a word processor requires a section break and the "different first page" setting, not manual deletion.

Anonymous marking

This is the error with real consequences. Many UK departments mark dissertations anonymously, which means your name must not appear anywhere on the submission, including the title page and including the document properties.

Students put their name on the title page out of habit, and in the strictest departments that is treated as a breach of the assessment regulations rather than a formatting slip. Check your handbook, and if anonymity applies, use your student number alone and check the file properties too, because the author field often carries your name automatically.

Unsure whether your department wants a name, a number, a declaration or a logo? Send your handbook through the academic guidance form and a tutor will read the submission regulations with you, so you can set the page up correctly once.

Fonts are worth one sentence. Use the same typeface as the body of the dissertation, at a slightly larger size for the title only. A title page set in a display font that appears nowhere else in the document reads as a cover rather than as part of the submission, and a handful of departments specify the typeface explicitly.

What does not belong on the title page

  • The abstract. It has its own page, immediately after.
  • The declaration of originality. Also its own page, unless the handbook explicitly places it on the front.
  • Acknowledgements. Their own page, after the abstract.
  • A table of contents. Comes later, and should be generated rather than typed.
  • Images or borders. A dissertation is not a report cover. Decoration reads as inexperience.
  • Your supervisor's name, usually. Only include it if asked, because in anonymous marking it can identify you.

Writing the title itself

The formatting is the easy half. The title is the part that is actually assessed, in the sense that it frames everything an examiner expects.

A strong dissertation title names the variables, the population and the context. "An investigation into employee motivation" tells a reader nothing. "Remote working policy and voluntary turnover in UK professional services firms, 2023 to 2026" tells them what was studied, in whom, where and when.

Three practical rules. Avoid abbreviations unless they are universally understood in your field. Avoid question marks, because most departments prefer a declarative title. And keep it under about twenty words, using a colon to separate a short conceptual phrase from a longer descriptive one if you need both.

If your title still names a broad subject rather than a bounded question, the problem is upstream of the title page, and our guidance on shaping a dissertation covers the narrowing work that fixes it.

How the title page differs at doctoral level

A thesis title page carries the same elements with two additions. The degree line usually states the full award, so "submitted for the degree of Doctor of Philosophy" rather than an abbreviation, and many institutions require a faculty as well as a department. Some also ask for the month and year of the viva rather than of submission, which is a distinction worth checking early because it affects the file you upload after corrections.

The other difference is the declaration. Doctoral regulations usually prescribe exact wording covering originality, the extent of collaboration and whether any part has been submitted elsewhere. That wording is not something to paraphrase. Copy it from the regulations, because it is a formal statement rather than a stylistic choice, and an approximation can delay an award while it is corrected.

A five minute check before submission

  1. Title matches the title on your submission portal entry exactly.
  2. Degree title matches the handbook wording exactly.
  3. No page number displayed, but the sequence is intact behind it.
  4. Name present or absent according to the anonymity rule, including in file properties.
  5. Word count shown if required, and accurate.
  6. One font, consistent spacing, no stray formatting from a template.

The first item is worth doing carefully. A title page that disagrees with the portal record creates an administrative query, and administrative queries at submission are the last thing anyone needs in that week.

Frequently asked questions

What goes on a dissertation title page?

The full title, your name or student number, the degree the work is submitted for, the institution and department, and the month and year of submission. Word count and supervisor name are included only where the handbook asks for them.

Should the dissertation title page be numbered?

It is counted in the page sequence as page i but displays no number. Achieving that needs a section break and the different first page setting in your word processor, rather than deleting the number manually.

Should I put my name on the dissertation title page?

Only if your department does not mark anonymously. Where anonymous marking applies, use your student number alone and check the document properties too, since the author field often carries your name automatically.

Can I add my university logo to the title page?

Only if your handbook permits it. Many institutions reserve their crest for official documents and some explicitly prohibit its use on student work, so check before adding one. Decoration generally reads as inexperience.

How long should a dissertation title be?

Under about twenty words, naming the variables, the population and the context. Use a colon to separate a short conceptual phrase from a longer descriptive one, avoid abbreviations, and prefer a declarative title to a question.
