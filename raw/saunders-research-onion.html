The research onion, unpicked layer by layer

A tutoring walk-through of Saunders' framework, then two filled examples so you can see the same model produce two very different methodologies.

Short answer: The research onion is a six-layer diagram by Saunders, Lewis and Thornhill that helps you design a dissertation methodology from the outside in. You work through philosophy, approach, strategy, methodological choice, time horizon, then techniques and procedures, so every decision at the centre (how you actually collect and analyse data) is justified by the layers around it. Peeling the research onion in order is what turns a vague plan into a defensible methodology chapter.

Layer 1: Research philosophy

Your assumptions about knowledge and reality

The outer skin sets your beliefs about what counts as valid knowledge. The common positions are positivism (observable, measurable facts), interpretivism (meaning constructed by people), realism and critical realism, and pragmatism (use whatever answers the question). Your philosophy quietly decides everything inside it, so name it early. If you want a deeper look at where philosophy sits in the whole chapter, our guide to research methodology maps the full structure.

Layer 2: Research approach

Deductive, inductive or abductive reasoning

This layer is the direction of your logic. Deductive research starts from theory and tests it with data, and tends to pair with positivism. Inductive research starts from data and builds theory, and tends to pair with interpretivism. Abductive reasoning moves back and forth. A frequent marker query here is whether your stated approach actually matches your questions, so make the link explicit.

Layer 3: Methodological strategy

The overall plan for answering the question

Strategy is the recognised research design you adopt: experiment, survey, case study, action research, grounded theory, ethnography, archival research or narrative inquiry. One dissertation can combine two, but each must earn its place. This is often the layer supervisors push on, because a weak strategy makes the whole methodology wobble.

Layer 4: Methodological choices

Mono-method, mixed method or multi-method

Here you decide whether the study is quantitative only, qualitative only, or a blend. Mono-method uses one; mixed methods combine qualitative and quantitative within one design; multi-method uses several of the same family. If you are still weighing this choice, our explainer on qualitative vs quantitative research works through the trade-offs for UK dissertations.

Layer 5: Time horizon

Cross-sectional or longitudinal

A cross-sectional study is a snapshot at one point in time, which suits most Masters dissertations given the tight window. A longitudinal study tracks change across time. Be realistic here: a longitudinal design that your submission deadline cannot support is a red flag to any examiner.

Layer 6: Techniques and procedures

Data collection and data analysis, the core

The centre is the practical detail: sampling, instruments (surveys, interviews, observation, secondary data), and how you analyse (statistical tests, thematic analysis, and so on). This is also where reliability and validity in research live. Reliability is about consistency, whether the same method would give the same result again. Validity is about accuracy, whether you measured what you claimed to measure. In quantitative work you will meet constructs such as face validity statistics, where you judge whether a measure looks, on its face, like it captures the concept before you run heavier statistical checks. Get reliability and validity in research right and the methodology stands up; skip it and markers notice fast.

Two worked examples: same onion, two methodologies

The best way to understand the research onion saunders designed is to fill it in. Below we peel the saunders research onion twice. Notice how the same six layers legitimately lead to two different data collection cores.

Example A: Masters Business dissertation

Research question: does flexible remote-working policy affect employee retention in UK mid-sized tech firms?

PhilosophyPositivism. The question assumes a measurable relationship between policy and retention that exists independently of the researcher.
ApproachDeductive. Start from retention theory, form a hypothesis, then test it against data.
StrategySurvey. A structured questionnaire distributed across several firms gives breadth and comparability.
ChoicesMono-method quantitative, so the analysis stays statistical and the sample can be large.
Time horizonCross-sectional. One survey wave inside the dissertation window, a defensible snapshot.
Techniques and proceduresStratified sample of employees; Likert-scale questionnaire; correlation and regression in SPSS. Reliability checked with Cronbach's alpha; validity supported by piloting and face validity statistics before the main run.

Example B: Masters Nursing dissertation

Research question: how do newly qualified nurses experience emotional support during their first year on a UK ward?

PhilosophyInterpretivism. The question is about lived meaning, which is constructed and personal, not a fixed external fact.
ApproachInductive. Let themes emerge from the nurses' own accounts rather than testing a prior hypothesis.
StrategyPhenomenology, a form of qualitative inquiry suited to first-person experience.
ChoicesMono-method qualitative, so depth is prioritised over breadth.
Time horizonCross-sectional. In-depth interviews across a few weeks, reflecting on the first year.
Techniques and proceduresPurposive sample of newly qualified nurses; semi-structured interviews; Vancouver referencing; thematic analysis. Here reliability and validity in research are framed as trustworthiness, credibility and an audit trail rather than statistical tests.

Two honest studies, one framework. Example A ends at regression tables; Example B ends at themes. Neither is more rigorous; each is justified by the layers above it. Framing terms that trip Masters students up

Two phrases cause confusion around the onion. The first is theoretical framework vs conceptual framework. A theoretical framework borrows an existing named theory to explain your topic. A conceptual framework is your own map of the concepts and how you expect them to link, often drawn from several sources. The onion is about method; the framework is about the ideas your method serves, so keep them in separate sections. The second is confusing strategy with choice: "case study" is a strategy, "qualitative" is a choice, and stating one does not settle the other.

Frequently asked questions

What is the research onion in simple terms?

The research onion is a six-layer diagram by Saunders, Lewis and Thornhill that helps you design a dissertation methodology from the outside in: philosophy, approach, strategy, methodological choice, time horizon, then techniques and procedures. Each outer layer justifies the ones inside it, so your final data collection method is fully reasoned.

Do I have to use the Saunders research onion in my Masters dissertation?

Not always, but many UK supervisors expect it because it structures the methodology chapter clearly. Even if you do not draw the diagram, working through its six layers is a reliable way to make sure every methodological decision is explained and justified.

What is the difference between reliability and validity in research?

Reliability is about consistency: would the same method give the same result if repeated? Validity is about accuracy: did you measure what you set out to measure? Quantitative studies test these statistically, including checks such as face validity statistics, while qualitative studies frame them as trustworthiness and credibility.

What is the difference between a theoretical framework vs conceptual framework?

A theoretical framework applies an existing named theory to explain your topic. A conceptual framework is your own map of the key concepts and expected relationships, often built from several sources. The research onion covers method, while these frameworks cover the ideas your method serves, so keep them in separate sections.
