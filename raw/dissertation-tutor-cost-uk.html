How dissertation tutoring is priced: the per-1,000-word model

Most credible UK providers, us included, guide dissertation support by volume of material rather than by a single flat fee. The unit is typically per 1,000 words of your draft that a tutor reads, annotates and discusses with you. This is fairer than a lump sum because a 10,000-word master's dissertation and a 3,000-word literature review need very different amounts of tutor time, and you should not pay the same for both.

Guiding by the 1,000-word unit also keeps the relationship transparent. You can start with a single chapter, see the quality of the feedback, and then decide whether to continue. There is no obligation to commit the whole dissertation up front, and no pressure to. The figure you are given is always a range, quoted in your currency, and confirmed after we understand the brief.

What moves the price of an online dissertation tutor

Two students can ask about the same 12,000-word dissertation and receive different ranges, because the work behind each is different. Here are the four factors that shift your quote the most.

1. Your academic level

Level is the biggest single driver. Undergraduate dissertation guidance is the entry band. A master's dissertation raises expectations on critical analysis, methodology and referencing depth, so it sits in the middle band. Doctoral-level work, with its original contribution and defence, sits highest. A tutor supporting a level they specialise in works faster and gives sharper feedback, which is what you are paying for.

2. How close the deadline is

A relaxed timeline lets a tutor schedule your chapters sensibly alongside other students. A compressed deadline means reserving focused blocks of a specialist's week at short notice, and that carries a premium. The earlier you engage an online dissertation tutor, the more control you have over cost. Last-minute reviews are possible, but they are quoted at the top of the range.

3. Subject and referencing style

Subject shifts the figure because specialist knowledge is not interchangeable. Business and management guidance draws on a large tutor pool. A psychology dissertation, a nursing study or a law dissertation needs a tutor fluent in that field and its conventions, including the right referencing style, whether Harvard, APA, OSCOLA or Vancouver. Narrower specialisms and stricter conventions tend to sit higher in the band.

4. Whether data analysis is involved

This is the factor students most often underestimate. A dissertation with a substantial quantitative or qualitative analysis component needs a tutor who can talk you through the method, the software output and the interpretation. Supporting SPSS, R, thematic analysis or a mixed-methods design takes more specialist time than a purely theoretical piece, so a data-heavy dissertation is quoted toward the upper band regardless of level.

How cost spreads across a dissertation's stages

One reason a lump sum feels intimidating is that it hides where the effort actually goes. When you work with an online dissertation tutor stage by stage, the cost spreads across the life of your project instead of landing all at once. Most students weight their guidance toward the methodology and analysis stages, where specialist input matters most, and lighten it around the proposal and final proofread.

Because you can engage stage by stage, you keep control. If your literature review is already strong but your methodology chapter is holding you back, you spend your budget there and nowhere else. This is very different from a single fixed price that assumes every chapter needs the same amount of help.
